Key Insight

The algorithm has two retrieval sources: in-network (accounts you follow) and out-of-network (the global corpus). Out-of-network content is how strangers discover your posts. That's the growth engine. The code calls it Phoenix Retrieval.

Chapter Two

The Score That Determines Your Fate

The algorithm uses a Grok-based transformer model called Phoenix. It doesn't guess what you want — it predicts the probability of you taking specific actions on each post, then multiplies those probabilities by weights to create one final score.

The Algorithm's Core Formula Final Score = Σ (weight × P(action))
Final Score =
  (w₁ × P(like)) + (w₂ × P(reply))
  + (w₃ × P(repost)) + (w₄ × P(share))
  − (w₅ × P(mute)) − (w₆ × P(block))
  − (w₇ × P(report))

Every action the algorithm predicts is weighted. Positive actions (likes, replies, reposts) push your score up. Negative actions (mute, block, report, "not interested") push your score down.

The Trap Most Creators Fall Into

Bait posts that trigger "not interested" or silent unfollows are algorithmically devastating. One wave of negative signals can suppress your content for weeks. The algorithm remembers. Stop chasing impressions at the expense of quality signal.

Notice what's not in the formula: raw impressions, follower count, post frequency. The algorithm is built on engagement quality, not vanity metrics. This is both the danger and the opportunity for serious builders.

Chapter Three

What Gets You Killed vs. What Gets You Boosted

Before scoring even begins, a set of hard filters run. These are automatic disqualifications — no ML needed, no score calculated. Your post simply doesn't exist for the viewer if it trips one of these.

☠ Instant Kills (Pre-Scoring Filters)
  • Post is older than the age threshold
  • Post from a blocked or muted account
  • Post contains muted keywords
  • Post already served in this session
  • Duplicate post (same content ID)
  • Post is behind a subscription paywall you don't have
  • Post flagged: spam, violence, gore, deleted
  • Self-posts removed from your own FYF
⚡ What the System Actively Wants
  • High probability of reply (most weighted signal)
  • High probability of repost or quote
  • Causing profile clicks and follows
  • Posts generating dwell time
  • Content semantically similar to what user engages with
  • Out-of-network posts that match user embedding
  • Author with consistent positive signals
  • Verified/premium account status

Chapter Four

The Two-Tower Model: Your Growth Engine

Most creators only think about the followers they already have. That's the in-network game (called Thunder internally). But the real growth lever is the out-of-network engine — the part called Phoenix Retrieval.

Phoenix uses a Two-Tower Model to find strangers who should see your content:

How Two-Tower Works

Tower 1 — User Tower: Encodes a specific user's engagement history into a mathematical vector (embedding). What they liked, replied to, shared — compressed into numbers that represent their interests.

Tower 2

Tower 2 — Candidate Tower: Encodes your post into a similar vector. The algorithm then calculates the dot product similarity between user vectors and post vectors to find matches. If your post's embedding is close to a non-follower's interest embedding, they see you.

This is the mechanism behind going viral to people who don't follow you. The algorithm is doing high-dimensional similarity matching at scale — millions of vectors, sub-second latency.

Practical Implication

Your content needs to be topically coherent and consistent. The system builds a representation of what your account is about based on your post history. If you tweet about 12 different things, your embedding is blurry. If you own one niche, your embedding is sharp — and the algorithm sends you to the right non-followers.

This is why niche accounts grow faster than generalist accounts on X, all else being equal. It's not an opinion — it's mathematics.

Chapter Five

The Business Hustle Playbook

Now we translate engineering into action. Fourteen rules derived directly from the source code — not speculation, not guru advice.

  • 01
    Engineer for replies, not likes Replies are the highest-weighted positive signal in the algorithm. End posts with questions. Create controversy worth engaging with. A post with 50 replies beats one with 500 likes in scoring.
  • 02
    Own a niche. Your embedding depends on it. The Two-Tower model builds a vector for your account. Mixed content = blurry vector = low out-of-network reach. Pick a lane. Business, tech, civic, finance — one sharp topic signals stack.
  • 03
    Never post content that invites blocking or muting P(block) and P(report) carry large negative weights. One viral post that triggers mass muting can suppress your account for weeks. Controversy has to earn replies, not repulsion.
  • 04
    Fresh content beats archived wisdom The age filter removes posts older than a threshold. Evergreen threads posted once and never refreshed will decay. Re-share, re-frame, and recycle your best ideas on a schedule.
  • 05
    Make people click your profile P(profile_click) is a positive signal. A compelling post hook that makes someone curious about who wrote it is doing double work — engagement on the post and a signal that your account is worth knowing.
  • 06
    Dwell time is real — write for it P(dwell) is tracked. Longer reads, threads, and media that require viewing time earn dwell signals. Don't over-optimise for short punchy takes at the expense of deep content that makes people stop scrolling.
  • 07
    Protect your engagement history The algorithm builds your "user action sequence" from what you've liked, replied to, and shared. Your engagement pattern on X is your taste fingerprint. It shapes what you see — and who you're shown to. Engage strategically.
  • 08
    Batch your posting, not your audience The Author Diversity Scorer penalises feeds dominated by one account. Don't flood your followers' timelines — you'll get attenuated. Quality over frequency. One high-signal post beats five mediocre ones algorithmically.
  • 09
    Reposts from high-signal accounts are gold P(repost) is highly weighted. Get one influential account to repost you and the cascade effect is real — because that repost then gets scored for all their followers' feeds.
  • 10
    Premium verification isn't just optics Verified/premium status is a feature in the hydration stage. The algorithm explicitly uses this as metadata. If you're serious about building on X, the subscription cost is an algorithm investment, not a vanity purchase.
  • 11
    Muted keywords don't exist — use them The MutedKeywordFilter removes content with keywords users have muted. Know your audience's mute lists. Political slurs, overused buzzwords, certain hashtags — if your target audience mutes them, your post vanishes for them.
  • 12
    Session recency matters PreviouslyServedPostsFilter kills posts already shown in a session. Don't expect your followers to see the same content twice in one scroll. Each session is a fresh competition for feed slots.
  • 13
    Replies and quote posts are distribution, not just engagement When you quote-post or reply to a viral post, you enter the candidate pool for everyone viewing that thread. The Phoenix retrieval engine can pull your content into feeds of users who've never seen you — via thread association.
  • 14
    There is no hack — there is only signal The code is explicit: "We have eliminated every single hand-engineered feature." The Grok transformer learned from billions of real human choices. You cannot trick it. You can only earn it.

Chapter Six

Your 30-Day Algorithm Reset

Close the book. Open your X profile. Run this audit against what you find.

  • Define your single niche topic — if you can't describe it in 5 words, you don't have one yet
  • Audit your last 20 posts: what percentage end with a question or clear reply hook?
  • Identify your last 3 posts that got zero replies — what killed the conversation?
  • Clean up who you engage with — your like history shapes your embedding
  • Set a content cadence: 3–5 posts per week, not 3–5 per day
  • Create one long-form thread per week designed for dwell time
  • Identify 5 high-signal accounts in your niche to engage with authentically
  • Review your bio and pinned post — do they make someone want to click your profile?
  • Decide: is X Premium worth the algorithm signal boost for your business? Run the maths.
  • Commit to 90 days of consistent niche content before judging results
